Pilates and lumbar disc herniation or bulge

This is how FlowMat builds pilates classes for a student with lumbar disc herniation or bulge. They are the app's real rules, reviewed by a medical committee: what isn't recommended is never suggested, and what needs care comes with a warning for the teacher.

Guidance for the class

Keep your spine neutral: avoid over-extending or over-flexing your lower back, and progress carefully if you feel discomfort.

Exercises that come with a warning

Examples of exercises that do appear, with the guidance the teacher gets.

CarefulWall roll down

Only roll down as far as you can control your abs, and stop if you feel pain or tension in your lower back.

CarefulAssisted roll up

Only roll back halfway while keeping your pelvis neutral, and stop if you feel pain or leg radiation.

CarefulSpine stretch forward

Keep your lower back long and stable, starting the curve from your head without forcing your low back.

CarefulSwan prep

Use a minimal extension range and keep the abdominals strongly engaged to protect the disc.

CarefulDead bug

Use the easy variation, lowering only the heel with the knee bent, without straightening the leg.

CarefulSpine twist

Rotate only your trunk without moving your pelvis, and avoid arching your lower back.

CarefulSupine hamstring stretch

Keep the pelvis neutral, avoid pulling the leg forcefully, and prioritize the band variation.

CarefulSupine leg press with band

Keep your lower back pressed into the mat and use a smaller hip-flexion range.

CarefulDonkey kick

Keep your pelvis neutral and lift your leg only as far as you can control without arching your lower back.

CarefulRoll back with ball

Keep the rocking limited to the ball's support and stop if you feel sharp pain or leg radiation.

CarefulThe Bug

Avoid rounding the low back; bend the knees more if you feel lumbar tension.

CarefulBand Kickback

Keep your pelvis stable without arching your lower back, and stop if you feel pain or leg radiation.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do pilates with lumbar disc herniation or bulge?

It depends on each person and on what their treating doctor says. FlowMat does not replace that judgment: it helps the teacher plan the class, avoiding what isn't recommended with lumbar disc herniation or bulge and flagging what needs care.

What does FlowMat do with exercises that are not recommended?

It never suggests them when building the class, and they don’t show up as a replacement when you swap an exercise either.

Can I change the class FlowMat builds?

Yes. You can swap, remove, add or reorder exercises and adjust the reps. The catalog only offers exercises that are safe for the student.
