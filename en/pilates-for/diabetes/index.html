Pilates and diabetes

This is how FlowMat builds pilates classes for a student with diabetes. They are the app's real rules, reviewed by a medical committee: what isn't recommended is never suggested, and what needs care comes with a warning for the teacher.

Guidance for the class

Before starting, ask whether they have eaten and have something sweet at hand; if trembling, cold sweat or dizziness appear, stop the class (possible low blood sugar).

Exercises FlowMat does not suggest

No exercise is ruled out for this condition: the care is in how each one is done. FlowMat suggests them with a warning and, when one exists, a gentler variation.

Exercises that come with a warning

Examples of exercises that do appear, with the guidance the teacher gets.

CarefulFoot and Ankle Strengthener

Pay attention to foot sensation and check your skin after the exercise.

CarefulToe and Heel Walk

Check your feet if you have neuropathy, and stop if you feel numbness or discomfort.

CarefulPlantar fascia release with small ball

Pay attention to foot sensation and avoid excessive pressure if you have neuropathy.

CarefulStanding foot awakening

Check your feet if you have neuropathy and stop if you notice any loss of sensation.

How the class is adjusted

  • 15 seconds of rest between exercises.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do pilates with diabetes?

It depends on each person and on what their treating doctor says. FlowMat does not replace that judgment: it helps the teacher plan the class, avoiding what isn't recommended with diabetes and flagging what needs care.

What does FlowMat do with exercises that are not recommended?

It never suggests them when building the class, and they don’t show up as a replacement when you swap an exercise either.

Can I change the class FlowMat builds?

Yes. You can swap, remove, add or reorder exercises and adjust the reps. The catalog only offers exercises that are safe for the student.
