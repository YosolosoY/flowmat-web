Pilates and pregnancy (second and third trimester)

This is how FlowMat builds pilates classes for a student with pregnancy (second and third trimester). They are the app's real rules, reviewed by a medical committee: what isn't recommended is never suggested, and what needs care comes with a warning for the teacher.

Guidance for the class

Avoid lying face down and do not spend more than a few minutes in a row on your back; if dizziness, bleeding, pain or contractions appear, stop the class and consult their midwife or doctor.

Exercises that come with a warning

Examples of exercises that do appear, with the guidance the teacher gets.

CarefulHundred prep

Prop your torso up 30° with a pillow and shorten time on your back; change position if you feel dizzy.

CarefulAssisted roll up

Only lower as far as you can control without pressing the belly, and breathe without holding your breath.

CarefulSingle leg stretch

Prop your torso up with a pillow and rest your head on the floor if your neck feels strained.

CarefulShoulder bridge

If you're in late pregnancy, try the supported bridge with a block under the sacrum, without actively holding the position.

CarefulRoll back with band

Only roll halfway back and avoid staying flat on your back for long; change position if you feel dizzy.

CarefulRoll back with ball

Use the ball as your limit and avoid lying flat on your back for long; stop if you feel dizzy.

CarefulThe Bug

Reduce the forward lean and leave room for the belly; avoid holding the position too long.

CarefulFloor Adductor Stretch with Band

If you're in later pregnancy, limit time on your back and roll to one side if you feel dizzy or pressure.

CarefulCross-Legged Forward Bend

In the second or third trimester, keep the torso more upright and avoid folding deeply over the belly.

CarefulReclined Butterfly

If you feel dizzy or heavy lying on your back, prop your torso up or turn onto your side.

CarefulSupine marching

In the second or third trimester, prop your torso up with pillows or do the seated version against a wall.

CarefulKneeling hip flexor stretch

Use a chair or wall for balance and don't force the stretch.

How the class is adjusted

  • Reps and times at 80% of normal.
  • 20 seconds of rest between exercises.
  • It asks for medical clearance if it is high risk.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do pilates with pregnancy (second and third trimester)?

It depends on each person and on what their treating doctor says. FlowMat does not replace that judgment: it helps the teacher plan the class, avoiding what isn't recommended with pregnancy (second and third trimester) and flagging what needs care.

What does FlowMat do with exercises that are not recommended?

It never suggests them when building the class, and they don’t show up as a replacement when you swap an exercise either.

Can I change the class FlowMat builds?

Yes. You can swap, remove, add or reorder exercises and adjust the reps. The catalog only offers exercises that are safe for the student.
