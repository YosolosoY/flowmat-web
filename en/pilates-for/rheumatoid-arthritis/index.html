Pilates and rheumatoid arthritis

This is how FlowMat builds pilates classes for a student with rheumatoid arthritis. They are the app's real rules, reviewed by a medical committee: what isn't recommended is never suggested, and what needs care comes with a warning for the teacher.

Guidance for the class

During flare-ups, do not load inflamed joints and work gently; take special care of hands and wrists.

Exercises FlowMat does not suggest

No exercise is ruled out for this condition: the care is in how each one is done. FlowMat suggests them with a warning and, when one exists, a gentler variation.

Exercises that come with a warning

Examples of exercises that do appear, with the guidance the teacher gets.

CarefulFront Curls

If your wrists or elbows are inflamed, use very light weights or work without any.

CarefulZip-ups

Use very light weights and avoid gripping hard if your hands hurt.

CarefulSide Curls

Use very light weights and avoid gripping hard with your fingers.

CarefulGentle Quadruped Rotation

Support hands and knees on a soft surface and reduce loading time if there is inflammation.

CarefulFoot and Ankle Strengthener

Use a low-resistance band and stop if the foot joints hurt.

CarefulPlantar fascia release with small ball

Reduce pressure if the foot joints are inflamed.

CarefulLateral Flexion in Quadruped

Rest hands and knees on a soft surface and reduce loading time if inflamed.

CarefulSeated wrist circles

On flare-up days, reduce range and speed; stop if the joints hurt.

CarefulQuadruped thoracic rotation with side reach

Support hands and knees on a soft surface and shorten loading time if there is inflammation.

CarefulSeated Chair Wrist and Forearm Stretch

On flare-up days, reduce stretch intensity and avoid any pulling on the hand joints.

CarefulSeated Foot and Toe Stretch with Chair Support

On flare-up days, move the toes without pressing or forcing the finger joints.

How the class is adjusted

  • Reps and times at 80% of normal.
  • 20 seconds of rest between exercises.

Frequently asked questions

Can you do pilates with rheumatoid arthritis?

It depends on each person and on what their treating doctor says. FlowMat does not replace that judgment: it helps the teacher plan the class, avoiding what isn't recommended with rheumatoid arthritis and flagging what needs care.

What does FlowMat do with exercises that are not recommended?

It never suggests them when building the class, and they don’t show up as a replacement when you swap an exercise either.

Can I change the class FlowMat builds?

Yes. You can swap, remove, add or reorder exercises and adjust the reps. The catalog only offers exercises that are safe for the student.
